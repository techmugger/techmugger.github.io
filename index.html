About

I am a second-year M.Tech student in Cybersecurity. My research so far has two threads: watermarking deep neural networks so an owner can prove a model is theirs and identify who leaked it, and network intrusion detection for medical and industrial devices.

In both threads I try to make the evaluation harder on myself than the original papers did. I extended a published DNN watermarking scheme from two traceable users to twenty, and I reproduced a published IoMT intrusion detector to find out which of its reported gains came from the model and which came from data handling.

GAN-PCPT: A Scalable Framework for DNN Watermarking, Active Authorisation, and Blockchain-Anchored Traceability Under Adaptive Adversaries

Devaraju Gunturu and Geetha K, School of Computing, SASTRA Deemed University

Extends the PCPT/ACPT scheme of Fan et al. (J. Inf. Secur. Appl., 2025). A conditional GAN replaces video-frame triggers, so each user gets a private, reproducible trigger set from a seed and the scheme no longer depends on captured footage. The paper evaluates a live ambiguity attack, formalises ownership disputes as a seven-transaction blockchain lifecycle, and reports a model-extraction attack that defeats this watermark family as an open limitation.

In preparationJournal manuscript, 2026

QMV-Fusion: Quantile-Normalised Multi-View Learning for Leakage-Aware Intrusion Detection in the Internet of Medical Things

SASTRA Deemed University

Reproduces a published two-view TabNet detector on CICIoMT2024 and finds that, run from its printed description, it reaches only about 68% and 67% on the six-class and 19-class tasks. Leakage-aware preprocessing, per-view quantile transforms and balanced-accuracy early stopping close the gap in a third to a seventh of the training time. A tuned XGBoost on all 45 features matches the fusion model, and changing the evaluation protocol alone moves macro-F1 by five to eight points.
